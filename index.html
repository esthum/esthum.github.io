About

An evolutionary perspective on health.

My research sits at the intersection of evolutionary medicine, human biology, and psychoneuroimmunology. I'm especially interested in how temporally organized physiological processes—and differences among individuals—shape vulnerability to disease.

I completed my PhD in Biomedical Anthropology at Baylor University, where I studied longitudinal relationships among cortisol regulation, psychological outcomes, and traumatic experiences in Dominica. I now work in the Pontzer Lab at Duke University and coordinate the Population Ecology, Aging, and Health Network (PEcAHN).

Research

Areas of interest

01 /

Stress physiology & mental health

Using longitudinal data from Dominica to investigate how developmental patterns of endocrine regulation relate to later psychological responses to trauma, with particular attention to variation across symptom domains.

02 /

Wearable biosensors & physiological dynamics

Applying continuous field-based measurements of cortisol, inflammatory biomarkers, and melatonin to study physiological coordination over time and its relationship to human health.

03 /

Comparative human biology

Through PEcAHN, I support collaborative, multi-site research on ecology, aging, and health across diverse communities, including cross-site research coordination and data harmonization.
